Lil Wayne is called Weezy as a play on Wayne, the back half of his given name, Dwayne Michael Carter Jr. He dropped the D himself, telling CBS News in 2009 he did not want to carry the name of a father who was never in his life.

That is the short version. The long version is that Lil Wayne has more names than most rappers have songs, and unlike almost everyone else in this business, he has actually explained a lot of them on camera. Here is the whole roster, sorted by how well it holds up.

## What is Lil Wayne's real name, and why did he drop the D?

Dwayne Michael Carter Jr., born September 27, 1982, in New Orleans. The Jr. is the whole story.

In a CBS News special with Katie Couric that aired February 4, 2009, Wayne laid it out with zero decoration: "I'm a junior and my father is livin' ... he's never been in my life, so I don't wanna be Dwayne." Couric asked whether his father knew about the edit. Wayne's answer was four words: "He knows now."

It is one of the most economical acts of self-authorship in rap. Most rappers pick a name to sound tougher or richer. Wayne removed a single letter to delete a person. Everything downstream of that decision — Wayne, Weezy, Weezy F Baby, Tunechi — is built on a name he chose for himself at a very young age.

## Why is Lil Wayne called Weezy?

Weezy is the one nickname Wayne has never sat down and explained, which is funny given how thoroughly he has documented the rest. What we can say is what the record shows: it is an obvious stretch of Wayne, it has been in circulation since the Cash Money era, and by 2005 it was solid enough to title a song. "Weezy Baby" is track seventeen on *Tha Carter II*.

Anyone who tells you they know the exact afternoon someone first said it is guessing. Nicknames that stick usually do not have origin stories. They have friends who kept saying them until they became load-bearing.

That is worth stating plainly, because a lot of pages will confidently hand you a Weezy origin myth. Wayne has explained Tunechi. He has explained Shrimp Daddy. He has explained the D. He has never explained Weezy, and the honest read is that he never had to, because it explains itself the second you say Dwayne out loud.

The "Lil," meanwhile, is not a mystery at all. Wayne signed to Cash Money at eleven years old. He was, factually, the little one. That is the same logic behind [half the "Lil" names in rap history](/why-rappers-lil), and Wayne is the artist who turned the prefix from a description into a franchise.

## What does the F in Weezy F Baby stand for?

Everything. That is not a dodge; it is the design.

Wayne introduced the format in 2003 on "Da Drought": "My name happens to be Weezy F. Baby. The F is for you to find out, man." He then spent the next two decades answering his own riddle differently every time. Complex assembled a running catalogue of them, and the clean highlights alone are a career: Fly (2003), Fresh (2004), Flow (2004), FEMA (2005), Fashion (2009), Ferocious (2009), Franchise (2009), Forever (2009), Forensics (2009), Free (2011), Finisher (2011), Fascinate (2011), Fortunate (2013).

It is the best structural joke in rap. He built a nickname with a blank in it, and the blank became the punchline generator. No other rapper has a name that renews itself every verse.

Look at the clustering and you can watch a mixtape run happen in real time. There are a handful of F's spread across 2003 to 2005, then the count detonates in 2009 and 2011 — exactly the stretch when Wayne was on every remix in America and needed a fresh opener four times a week. The nickname was not a brand exercise. It was a working rapper solving the same setup problem over and over and refusing to repeat himself.

There is also a whole tier of F's we are not printing here, because Wayne's version of this game gets extremely unprintable extremely fast. Assume the catalogue is bigger and bluer than the sample above.

The most quoted one is also the one that was reportedly a mistake. "Weezy F Baby and the F is for Phenomenal," from 2009's "Yes," treats an F as a stand-in for a "ph" sound. Wayne's longtime manager Cortez Bryant has said in interviews that Wayne was annoyed by the error and wanted it changed, viewing it as a misspelling rather than wordplay. The internet, correctly, overruled him. Note that this account comes from Bryant, not from Wayne directly.

## Why is Lil Wayne called Tunechi?

This one is genuinely well sourced, which is rare enough to be worth flagging.

The story you see repeated everywhere — that his grandmother named him — is true, and it is true because Wayne said it on camera. In a February 2010 interview with DJ Scoob Doo, he explained: "My nickname is Lil Tune — my grandmother named me that. She's passed. My grandmother, her name was Mercedes Carter. She named me Lil Tune. We just threw the 'Chi' on there. Like Gucci, I say 'Tunechi!'"

So: Lil Tune is the inherited part. The "-chi" is Wayne's own flourish, admittedly modelled on Gucci. His first public use of Tunechi came on "Run This Town," from the mixtape *No Ceilings*, released October 31, 2009.

Take the specificity seriously. He names his grandmother. He explains which half he added. Compare that to the average rap etymology, which is usually a decade-old forum post that hardened into fact, and Tunechi looks bulletproof.

## Why is Lil Wayne called Birdman Jr.?

Because he was raised in the building, and both parties said so loudly for about fifteen years.

Cash Money co-founder Birdman discovered Wayne in 1993 and signed him at eleven. Wayne's stepfather, Reginald "Rabbit" McDonald, was killed in 1997, which deepened Birdman's role in his life. By 2006 the father-son framing was not subtext, it was a marketing plan: the two released a joint album titled *Like Father, Like Son* on October 31, 2006, led by the single "Stuntin' Like My Daddy." It debuted at number three on the Billboard 200 with 176,000 copies sold and went gold that December.

Birdman put it flatly the same year: "Wayne to me is my son, my first-born son, and that's what it do for me."

The two later spent years in a public business dispute that settled in 2018, and Birdman performed with Wayne at Lil Weezyana Fest that August. The nickname survived all of it, which tells you something about how early names get lodged.

## Shrimp Daddy, Baby D, and the names that did not survive

Before any of this, there was Shrimp Daddy. Wayne told Nardwuar the Human Serviette exactly how that happened: "Shrimp Daddy. And I named myself because I was short, so I took the shrimp and I wanted to be like this guy right here, Pimp Daddy." Pimp Daddy was a New Orleans artist on Cash Money. Wayne, being small, downsized the title accordingly.

It is a perfect rap name origin: aspiration filtered through honest self-assessment.

He then went through Baby D as half of the duo the B.G.'z with B.G., who released *True Story* in 1995. As The FADER put it in a 2017 oral history of his early New Orleans years, "that was Wayne, but they didn't call him Wayne at that time. He was known as Baby D."

The later aliases are more like job titles than nicknames. President Carter and Mr. Carter both lean on his surname, which also anchors the *Tha Carter* album series — a run that peaked with *Tha Carter III* in 2008, which moved 1,005,545 copies in its first week, the first album to clear a million in seven days since 50 Cent's *The Massacre* in 2005. When you sell like that, you get to promote yourself to president.

Worth clearing up a common mix-up: Mack Maine is not one of Wayne's nicknames. Jermaine Anthony Preyan, better known as Mack Maine, is a New Orleans rapper and longtime Wayne collaborator who has been president of Young Money Entertainment since 2009. Wayne founded Young Money in 2005. Different person, same building.

## Will Lil Wayne ever drop the Lil?

There is a widely repeated claim that Wayne has said he wants to. We could not find a verifiable statement from him to that effect, and until one surfaces, treat it as unconfirmed.

What is on the record is what his peers did. Bow Wow dropped his in April 2002 and told MTV News why: "I changed my name because I'm getting older now and it's too many Lil's ... Besides, I'm growing up, I'm not little anymore." Romeo Miller had dropped his by 2006. Both were child stars who aged out of the diminutive.

Wayne never did, and at this point the word does not mean small anymore. It means him. That is the reward for getting there first and staying the longest.
