Gucci Mane's "Brrr" is the sound of being cold, a nod to the diamond imagery of his 2005 breakout "Icy." The 1017 in 1017 Records is an address: his grandfather's house at 1017 First Avenue in Bessemer, Alabama, per Gucci's own memoir.

Two questions, one man, and one of the answers is not what the internet thinks it is. Let us take them in order.

## What "Brrr" actually means

It is a shiver. That is the whole translation.

The reading everyone agrees on, and the one Revolt has published, connects the sound to cold, and cold in rap means one thing: jewelry. Ice. Diamonds. Gucci Mane's first hit was literally called "Icy," released April 13, 2005 as the lead single from his debut album *Trap House*, produced by Zaytoven and featuring Young Jeezy and Boo. The song charted on Hot Rap Songs and Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs and made him a name outside Atlanta. Everything about his early branding pointed at temperature.

So "Brrr" is not a coded word. It is a sound effect that happens to be a mission statement.

## What is not documented about "Brrr"

Here is where most articles start inventing things.

Gucci Mane has never published a detailed, on-record origin story for the ad-lib. There is no interview where he dates the first "Brrr," names the session, or explains the decision. The cold-and-diamonds reading is widely reported and it fits the evidence perfectly, but it is interpretation, not testimony. Anyone telling you the exact studio where it was born is filling in a blank.

What we can verify is the surrounding architecture. He leaned into the frozen-villain image hard enough to put an ice cream cone on his face in 2011. In his memoir he framed that tattoo as a deliberate choice, writing that he wanted to embrace being the villain rather than argue with anyone's opinion of him. That is a man who understood his own brand years before most of his peers understood theirs.

## The snowman thing is a different rapper

This is the most common mix-up in the whole conversation, so let us settle it.

The Snowman logo was **Jeezy's**, not Gucci's. Designer Eif Rivera created the graphic, it spread as merch around the July 2005 release of *Let's Get It: Thug Motivation 101*, and by that fall school districts in Tennessee, Alabama and New York had banned the shirt. It became a national news cycle.

Gucci and Jeezy were on "Icy" together in 2005 and then spent well over a decade at odds over that record, a feud they finally retired by performing the song side by side at Verzuz in Atlanta in November 2020. Producer Zaytoven later said Jeezy had tried to buy the track outright for his own debut and that he turned it down.

Two Atlanta artists, one shared song, and two completely separate cold-weather brands. The snowman is Jeezy's. The shiver is Gucci's. Anyone merging them is remembering 2005 through a fog.

## Why do rappers say "Brrr" at all?

Because it is the most efficient signature available.

An ad-lib has to survive being played on a phone speaker in a parking lot. It cannot rely on nuance. The best ones are short, loud, non-verbal and impossible to mistake for anyone else, which is exactly the logic behind [Rick Ross and his one-syllable grunt](/rick-ross-huh). A shiver clears every bar. No language required, no context required, and it sits in a frequency range that does not fight the 808.

Once Gucci proved it worked, the shiver went general-issue across trap, the way a fashion detail gets copied until nobody remembers who wore it first. We break down the full mechanics of why these sounds function the way they do in our [guide to rap ad-libs](/rap-ad-libs-explained).

## What 1017 actually means

Not a date. An address.

In *The Autobiography of Gucci Mane*, he writes that he came up in his granddaddy's house at 1017 First Avenue, and that the address is where the label and management company names came from. Andscape's profile describes the place in detail: an olive-green two-bedroom near the train tracks in Bessemer, Alabama, the town where Radric Delantic Davis was born on February 12, 1980 before the family moved to East Atlanta when he was nine.

Gucci has said the same thing publicly in his own words. In a social media post he wrote that 1017 started in Bessemer, Alabama, and paid respects to his late grandfather Walter Davis Sr. and his uncle Walter Davis Jr. His grandfather died of a heart attack when Gucci was seven, a loss Andscape reports split the family.

So the most tattooed number in modern rap is a mailbox in Alabama belonging to a man who never heard a single one of these records. That is a better story than any of the invented ones.

## From So Icey to 1017 Brick Squad to The New 1017

The company itself has changed clothes several times.

Gucci established the operation in 2007 after leaving Mizay Entertainment. It ran as **So Icey Entertainment** from 2007 until it closed in May 2010, then relaunched as **1017 Brick Squad**, with OJ da Juiceman and Waka Flocka Flame as the first signees. The relaunch followed a period of incarceration.

After his release it became **1017 Eskimo** through a partnership with Alamo and EMPIRE. In 2020 it rebranded again as **The New 1017**, this time with Atlantic Records. The roster across those eras reads like a scouting report on the last fifteen years of Southern rap: Young Thug, Waka Flocka Flame, OJ da Juiceman, Pooh Shiesty, Big Scarr, Enchanting. As of November 2024 the active roster had narrowed to Pooh Shiesty and Foogiano.

Four names, one number, and the number never moved.

## So why does everyone think 1017 means October 17?

Because Gucci turned the address into a calendar.

Once the number was the brand, the date became the holiday. Outlets covering the anniversary have noted his habit of anchoring drops and milestones to October 17, and in 2023 the Atlanta City Council formalized it, proclaiming October 17 **Gucci Mane Day**. Black Enterprise noted plainly that the date functions as an abbreviation of the label name.

That is the correct order of events, and it matters. The address came first, in the 1980s. The date is a nickname for the address, and it is roughly forty years younger. Anyone who tells you Gucci picked 1017 because of a birthday, a release date or a deal signing is reverse-engineering.

## Where the name "Gucci Mane" came from

Same principle, third example. The stage name is not his invention either. It came from his father, who was known as "the Gucci Man," and in his memoir Gucci notes he was only third in line for it: his father handed the nickname to a nephew before it ever reached Radric.

So the ad-lib is a sound effect, the label is a mailbox, and the stage name is a hand-me-down. Not one piece of the most recognizable brand in trap was manufactured in a meeting. That pattern runs through most of the [rap nicknames that last](/rapper-nicknames-explained): the good ones are found, not chosen.

## Why any of this sticks

Gucci Mane built an identity out of two things that cost nothing: a sound with no words and a number with no explanation. Neither requires a marketing budget. Both are unforgeable, because you cannot copy a shiver without sounding like a guy copying a shiver, and you cannot claim somebody else's grandfather's address.

That is the same trick that runs underneath every [producer tag in modern rap](/producer-tags-explained). Give people something short enough to repeat and specific enough to belong to only you, and you never have to introduce yourself again.

He was doing brand strategy from a two-bedroom in Bessemer before the phrase existed. The shiver was free. The address was already his.
