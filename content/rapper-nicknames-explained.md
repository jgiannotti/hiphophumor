Rap produces more aliases per capita than any genre because it inherited naming from graffiti tags and DJ handles, where the name was the reputation. Almost every rap nickname traces to one of six sources: a childhood name, an alter ego, a flex, a crew, a shortened birth name, or a mentor's gift.

Rock musicians get one stage name and keep it for forty years. Rappers get a name, outgrow it, get a second from a producer, invent a third to play a character, then let the internet assign a fourth. Below are 40, sorted by origin, with the folklore labeled as folklore.

## How Do Rappers Get Their Names?

Read a few hundred origin stories and the same six patterns keep surfacing. This is the working taxonomy.

**The childhood name that stuck.** The most common and least glamorous category. Somebody's mother, uncle or older cousin handed them a name at age six and they never escaped it. Snoop Dogg was Snoopy first. Gucci Mane inherited his father's nickname whole.

**The alter ego.** Not a second label for the same person, but a different character with a different voice. Nicki Minaj's Roman Zolanski has a hometown, an accent and a mother. MF DOOM built a career on the premise that the man and the villain were separate employees. If you can hear the change with your eyes closed, it is an alter ego.

**The flex name.** A luxury brand or status marker worn as a name. Saint Laurent Don. Cash Carti. Rozay. Nas Escobar. The grammar is almost always identical: an expensive noun plus a title of rank. It is a coat of arms, and it long predates the streetwear era.

**The crew or label name.** A name that only makes sense as part of a set. King Slime is meaningless alone and legible the moment you know the crew under it. Cactus Jack and 1017 work the same way: memberships more than identities.

**The shortened government name.** The simplest engine in the genre. Nasir becomes Nas. Kendrick becomes K.Dot. Chris becomes Ludacris. Kanye becomes Ye. It is the least romantic origin and by far the most frequent, which is why a surprising number of [rappers' real names](/rappers-real-names) are already most of the stage name.

**The mentor-given name.** The best stories live here, because somebody else's judgment is baked in. Chuck D named Busta Rhymes after a wide receiver. Magoo named Missy Elliott after a minor crime. A producer named Fendi renamed Onika Maraj. These names are inheritances, and artists discuss them the way people discuss a grandparent's watch.

A seventh category deserves naming: **the borrowed character.** Rap has raided kung-fu cinema, Marvel comics, mob movies and video games since the 1980s. ODB, Method Man, MF DOOM, Kung Fu Kenny and Frank White are all, finally, people who watched something and decided that was them now.

Two artists are so deep into this they need separate accounting: [Lil Wayne's alias collection](/lil-wayne-nicknames) and [the many names of Drake](/drake-nicknames) are less a list than a filing system.

## The Childhood Name That Stuck

Nine names assigned before their owners had any say in the matter.

### Why Is Snoop Dogg Called Snoop?

His mother called him Snoopy as a child because he looked like and loved the Peanuts character. Everything since extends that root: Snoop Doggy Dogg, then Snoop Dogg, then Snoop Lion in July 2012 after a Rastafari priest rechristened him in Jamaica, producing the 2013 reggae album Reincarnated. He has also recorded as Snoopzilla and DJ Snoopadelic, the latter a nod to George Clinton's Funkadelic. Nobody has renamed himself more and stayed himself more.

### Why Was Biggie Called Biggie Smalls?

Christopher Wallace was already "Big" at Westinghouse High School, where he weighed 201 pounds. He upgraded to Biggie Smalls, borrowed from Calvin Lockhart's character in the 1975 film Let's Do It Again, until a West Coast rapper who already held the name threatened to sue: "The original Biggy Smallz said he's going to sue me for everything I got if I use his name again." He settled on The Notorious B.I.G., explaining the acronym as "Business Instead of Game." Frank White comes from the 1990 film King of New York.

### Why Is Gucci Mane Called Guwop?

The Gucci part is not a shopping decision. It was his father's nickname, "the Gucci Man," and Radric Davis inherited it the way you inherit a middle name. Guwop is the same name sanded down by pronunciation, and it nearly became the main one: in 2013 he announced on Twitter that Gucci Mane was retiring and Guwop was taking over, then reversed himself almost immediately after fans revolted.

### Why Is Young Thug Called Thugger?

Thugger, and its doubled form Thugger Thugger, is what happens when a stage name gets used ten thousand times by people who are fond of you. Jeffery Williams has never explained the diminutive, and there is nothing to explain: it is the machinery that turns Michael into Mike, applied to a name that was already a persona. That it sounds affectionate rather than menacing is the point.

### Why Is Lil Baby Called Lil Baby?

Dominique Jones is commonly reported to have picked up the name as the youngest and smallest person in his circle, though he has not confirmed it and no primary source pins it down. What is documented is that he was among the last in his orbit to actually start rapping. The "Lil" prefix has [its own long history in rap](/why-rappers-lil) and almost always means what it sounds like.

### Why Is GloRilla Called GloRilla?

Gloria Woods, from Memphis, performs as GloRilla, and the obvious read is her first name fused with "gorilla." That is how the name is universally understood and it suits her delivery, which is all chest and no apology. It is also not something she has publicly confirmed, so treat the etymology as commonly reported rather than documented. Some names are self-explanatory enough that nobody thought to ask.

### Where Does the Name Sexyy Red Come From?

Janae Wherry, from St. Louis, adapted her stage name from a nickname she already had, with the "Red" tied to her dyed red hair. That is the entire origin, and its plainness is refreshing in a category full of elaborate mythology. The double Y is a spelling choice rather than a meaning, and it does what odd spellings are meant to do in the streaming era: make you findable.

### Why Is Ice Spice Called Ice Spice?

Isis Gaston went by Ice as a kid. The second half arrived at 14, when she needed a name for a private Instagram account and did the only sensible thing. As she told Hip Hop Nation: "What rhymes with ice? It was spice." A generation of rappers workshopped their names in cyphers. This one was workshopped in a handle field.

### Why Is Doja Cat Called Doja Cat?

Amala Dlamini assembled the name as a teenager around 2012. She has said the first half came from a slang term she thought sounded like a girl's name, and the second from one of her own cats. It is a bedroom-internet name, built in the same spirit as her early SoundCloud output, and she has occasionally sounded ambivalent about being stuck with it.

## The Government Name, Filed Down

Seven artists whose stage names are mostly their real names, edited.

### Why Is Cardi B Called Cardi B?

Belcalis Almanzar has a sister named Hennessy. People started calling her Bacardi, because siblings named after two liquors is a bit that writes itself. She told Jimmy Fallon in December 2017 that her Instagram account kept getting deleted, so she trimmed it: "I think it was Bacardi that had something to do with it. So I just shortened it to Cardi B."

### Why Is Kanye West Called Ye?

He released the album Ye in June 2018 and announced that September it was becoming his stage name. His stated reasoning, given to radio host Big Boy, was theological: "I believe 'ye' is the most commonly used word in the Bible. In the Bible it means you." The claim is wrong. "Lord" appears far more often in the King James Version. On October 18, 2021, a Los Angeles judge made Ye his legal name anyway.

### Why Was Kendrick Lamar Called K.Dot?

K.Dot was a high-school nickname that became a mixtape identity through the mid-2000s. He retired it around 2009 and started releasing music under his first and middle names, because he disliked how the alias pulled attention away from who he actually was. That decision looks obvious now. At the time, dropping a nickname for your government name was close to commercial malpractice.

### Why Is Ludacris Called Ludacris?

Christopher Bridges explained it himself in a Reddit AMA: "My first name is 'Chris' and if you look in the dictionary under the term 'ludicrous' then it means 'crazy, wild, ridiculous' - which pretty much explains everything about me." The better fact is what came before. At 10 or 11 he rapped as Kris Kringle, and quit after concluding that a career limited to Christmas material had a ceiling.

### Why Did Latto Change Her Name?

Alyssa Stephens spent 2016 onward performing under a longer version of the name, then announced in January 2021 that it was going. The change took effect that May, first appearing on a guest verse for an album released May 18, 2021. The reason was direct: sustained criticism that the original was colorist. She kept the sound and cut the part doing damage, which is the most efficient rebrand available to anyone.

### Why Is Central Cee Called Central Cee?

Oakley Caesar-Su, born in Ladbroke Grove and raised in Shepherd's Bush, first appears under the name on a 2014 episode of Charlie Sloth's Fire in the Streets, initially listed as Central C. The Cee almost certainly comes from Caesar-Su, though he has not spelled that out. The Central half is the genuinely unexplained part, given that he is from West London.

### Why Is Dave Called Santan Dave?

David Omoregie, born in Brixton and raised in Streatham, performs as Dave and Santan Dave. That is the whole documented record. One of the most decorated artists in British music, a Mercury Prize winner with a catalogue people write dissertations about, has a stage name whose second half carries no public explanation. Anyone telling you confidently where Santan comes from is guessing.

## The Name Somebody Else Gave You

Five names that arrived as gifts, which is why the stories are better.

### Why Is Busta Rhymes Called Busta Rhymes?

Chuck D of Public Enemy named him, and named him after George "Buster" Rhymes, a wide receiver who played in the NFL and CFL. Trevor Smith Jr. has spent more than thirty years as one of rap's most recognizable voices under a name lifted from a pro football roster by a man from another group. It remains the best argument for letting somebody else name you.

### Why Is Missy Elliott Called Misdemeanor?

Magoo gave it to her while they were recording "Beep Me 911" for her 1997 debut, and his reasoning was a compliment disguised as a charge sheet. Elliott shared it publicly in August 2023 in a tribute to him: "I'm going to call you Misdemeanor because it's a crime to have that many talents." Thirty years of Misdemeanor records, and the origin is one friend being kind to another in a studio.

### Why Is Metro Boomin Called Metro Boomin?

Two separate sources, welded. Metro comes from the bus line in his hometown of St. Louis. Boomin comes from OJ Da Juiceman, who kept describing Leland Wayne's beats as booming while the teenage producer spent weekends in Atlanta trying to get in the room. His producer tag is similarly traceable: it first appeared on Uncle Murda's "Right Now," voiced by Future, and went global via "Jumpman" in 2015.

### Why Is Lil Uzi Vert Called Lil Uzi Vert?

Symere Woods told the story in a 2016 Vibe interview: a stranger heard him rap, said his flow was fast, and compared it to a Uzi. The comparison became a name. Uzi later clarified on a 2021 livestream exactly who the stranger was, in terms that were affectionate and unprintable here. The "Vert" half has never been explained by anyone, including him.

### Why Is Travis Scott Called La Flame?

The main name is a splice: his favorite uncle was named Travis, and Scott is Kid Cudi's real first name. Jacques Webster built a stage name out of the two men he most wanted to be like, which is sweeter than the pyrotechnics suggest. La Flame and Cactus Jack, his best-known secondary names, have no documented origins on record, despite Cactus Jack now being a label.

## The Alter Ego

Seven characters, as opposed to seven nicknames. The distinction matters.

### Why Was 2Pac Called Makaveli?

Born Lesane Parish Crooks, he was renamed at age one after Tupac Amaru II, the Peruvian revolutionary executed in 1781. His mother, Afeni Shakur: "I wanted him to have the name of revolutionary, indigenous people in the world." Makaveli came from Niccolo Machiavelli, whose work he read while imprisoned. His own account: "My name is not Machiavelli. My name is Makaveli. I took it, that's mine." The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory took seven days to record in August 1996. The theory that Machiavelli faked his own death has no basis in fact.

### Why Is Nicki Minaj Called Roman?

The surname was not hers either: a producer known as Fendi swapped Onika Maraj's last name for Minaj, reportedly for her "nasty" flow. Roman Zolanski arrived later, fully formed. In the 2011 MTV documentary My Time Now she described him as "a crazy boy who lives in me and he says the things that I don't want to say," born out of rage. He is written as London-based, with a British accent and his own mother, Martha Zolanski.

### Why Is Nicki Minaj Called Harajuku Barbie?

Harajuku Barbie is the mixtape-era persona, fusing Tokyo's Harajuku fashion district with the doll, and it is the softest voice in her range. Chun-Li, introduced on a 2018 single that reached number ten in the US, shares its name with the Street Fighter character and plays closer to a wuxia villain than a video-game heroine. Between Roman, Martha, Barbie, Chun-Li and Nicki Lewinsky, she has run more distinct characters than most rappers have albums.

### Why Is Megan Thee Stallion Called Tina Snow?

The Stallion came first: in parts of the American South, tall and statuesque women get called stallions, and Megan Pete, at 5'10", was hearing it as a teenager. Tina Snow is a tribute rather than an invention, modeled directly on Pimp C's alter ego Tony Snow and introduced on her June 2018 EP of that name. Hot Girl Meg is the third setting, from Fever, and produced a catchphrase she trademarked in September 2019.

### Why Is Tyler, the Creator Called Wolf Haley?

The main name is unglamorous: he took Tyler, the Creator from a Myspace page he used to post his work. Wolf Haley is more interesting, because it is not really a rap persona. It is his directing credit. Every one of his music videos has been directed by Tyler under that pseudonym, making Wolf Haley less an alter ego than a second job title. Tyler Baudelaire, from 2021, is a full character: well-travelled and expensively insufferable.

### Why Is Nas Called Nasty Nas and Escobar?

Nasir Jones went by Kid Wave as a teenager before settling on Nasty Nas, the name he was working under by 1989 and using on his 1991 recording debut. Escobar arrived with It Was Written in 1996, borrowing a Colombian surname and dressing the persona in mob-movie tailoring. It was a deliberate pivot from documentary realism to widescreen fiction, it sold enormously, and it drew a decade of arguments from purists.

### Why Is Future Called Pluto (and Future Hendrix)?

The base name was a gift. G-Rock, of the Atlanta group Da Connect, told Nayvadius Wilburn he was the future of music, replacing the name he was reportedly using before, Meathead. Rico Wade, his first cousin and an Organized Noize founder, pulled him into the Dungeon Family orbit. Pluto comes from his April 2012 debut album and hardened into a persona he still uses. Future Hendrix was the working title of his second album before it became Honest. Neither the alien framing nor the Jimi Hendrix nod has a documented explanation from Future himself.

## The Borrowed Character

Five names lifted directly from a screen.

### Where Does the Name ODB Come From?

Russell Jones took his name from a 1980 Hong Kong martial-arts film starring Yuen Siu-tien, also released as An Old Kung Fu Master, whose alternate title is unprintable here. Method Man explained the name as meaning there was no father to his style, which is both a compliment and an accurate technical description: nobody had rapped like that before, and nobody has convincingly done it since.

### Why Is Method Man Called Method Man?

The best-documented origin is a 1979 Hong Kong martial-arts film carrying the same name in its US release, also known as The Fearless Young Boxer. That fits Wu-Tang's obsession with kung-fu cinema, which supplied names, samples and skit dialogue across the catalogue. Competing explanations circulate online, none sourced to Clifford Smith or RZA. His other alias, Johnny Blaze, comes from Marvel's Ghost Rider, a claim he later made literal by writing a Ghost Rider comic.

### Why Is MF DOOM Called DOOM?

Daniel Dumile was Zev Love X of KMD until his brother and groupmate DJ Subroc was killed by a car in April 1993, days before their second album was due. He disappeared for years, then resurfaced at Manhattan open mics performing with tights pulled over his face. The character he built was Marvel's Doctor Doom, reworked: a villain, masked, operating through intermediaries. The metal mask was later modeled on the one from the 2000 film Gladiator.

### Why Is Kendrick Lamar Called Kung Fu Kenny?

From Rush Hour 2. Don Cheadle plays a character named Kenny in the 2001 film, and Kendrick built an entire 2017 album-era visual identity around him. Cheadle did not know until April 2017, when he saw a tweet calling him the original Kung Fu Kenny and texted Kendrick to ask. Kendrick's reply, as Cheadle recounted it: "That's what the surprise was."

### Why Is Chief Keef Called Sosa?

Sosa is lifted from a character in the 1983 film Scarface, a movie that has probably supplied more rap aliases than any other single piece of media. Keef is the older and warmer half: Keith Cozart was named for his late uncle Keith Carter, known as Big Keef. The result is a stage name with a family memorial on one side and a Brian De Palma character on the other.

## The Flex, the Crew, and the Rebrand

Seven names built from money, membership, or a decision to start over.

### Why Is 21 Savage Called Saint Laurent Don?

The construction is textbook flex grammar: a French luxury house plus a mob honorific, the same recipe as Nas Escobar and Ricky Rozay. Shéyaa Bin Abraham-Joseph, born in Newham, London and raised in Atlanta from age seven, uses it as a social-media display name and a running self-reference. He has never publicly explained when or why he adopted it, so the Yves Saint Laurent connection, while obvious, is commonly reported rather than confirmed.

### Why Is Playboi Carti Called Cash Carti?

Jordan Carter started as Sir Cartier on SoundCloud in 2011, became Playboi Carti by 2013, and used Cash Carti across the same early period. All three run the same joke: his surname is Carter, which is one letter and a small amount of ambition away from Cartier. He has not documented the reasoning behind any of them, consistent with a career spent saying as little as possible.

### Why Is Rick Ross Called Rozay?

The stage name itself came from a 1980s Los Angeles figure he has no connection to. Rozay is the more interesting one, and the honest answer is that it is commonly read as a nod to rose champagne, though William Roberts has never given a documented origin. His third name is better sourced: in 2015 he started calling himself Renzel on Snapchat, a play on Denzel Washington.

### Why Is Jay-Z Called Hov and Jigga?

Shawn Carter was Jazzy around the neighborhood first, and Jay-Z is documented as an homage to his mentor Jaz-O. The story about the J and Z subway lines running near the Marcy Houses is repeated constantly and is not confirmed in the primary record, so file it under folklore. Hova is a contraction of Jay-Hova, a play on Jehovah that came from writing verses in under ten minutes.

### Why Is Young Thug Called King Slime?

King Slime only parses if you know the vocabulary underneath it. In his circle, slime functions as a term of endearment, closer to "brother" than to anything sinister, and it became the organizing word for a whole label and aesthetic. King Slime is the crown placed on top of that vocabulary. For the full etymology, [what slime means in rap](/what-does-slime-mean-in-rap) is longer and more contested than most people assume.

### Why Is Andre Benjamin Called Andre 3000?

Here is the honest answer: nobody knows, because he has never said. Andre Benjamin adopted the name around the end of the 1990s, before Stankonia, and the two standing theories are that he wanted daylight between himself and Dr. Dre, and that he was echoing Kool Keith's use of "3000." Both are speculation. One of the genre's most consequential rebrands has a completely blank origin field.

### Why Is Stormzy Called Stormzy?

Michael Omari, from Thornton Heath in South London, has been Stormzy since his teens and has never publicly explained it. That is genuinely unusual for an artist this documented: a Glastonbury headliner with two number-one albums and a publishing imprint, and the origin of his name is not reliably on the record anywhere. Sometimes a name is just the one your friends started using.

## The Pattern Underneath

The names that last are the ones with a person attached. Missy Elliott's came from a friend in a studio. Busta Rhymes' came from Chuck D. The self-assigned flex names age fastest, because a luxury brand is a rented signifier and every lease runs out.

Rappers are the last people in popular music who treat naming as a creative act rather than a legal formality. That is why this page will need updating by next year.
