In rap, "slime" is a term of endearment for a close friend, used the way most people use "my guy" or "bro." XXL credits New York rapper N.O.R.E. with bringing it into hip-hop around 1999. Young Thug later made it central to Atlanta rap, and King Slime is one of his documented stage names.

The interesting part is not the definition. It is that a word English has long used as an insult got turned into a compliment by a rapper from Queens, then adopted by a scene several states south, then absorbed by teenagers who have never heard either record.

## The word means the opposite of what it means

Merriam-Webster dates the adjective "slimy" to the 14th century and gives its figurative sense as **"vile, offensive."** That is a very long run of English agreeing that slime is bad.

Then rap did what rap does. Calling someone your slime is warm. It marks trust, proximity, and a shared history. Same word, inverted charge.

This is a well-worn mechanism, not a fluke. Taking a term with negative baggage and using it as praise creates an instant in-group signal, because outsiders hear the dictionary meaning and insiders hear the intended one. The friction is the feature.

## The New York chapter: N.O.R.E. and the Slime Father

XXL credits N.O.R.E. with putting the word into hip-hop, and he has never been shy about the claim. He nicknamed himself the Slime Father and staked his claim publicly on Twitter in 2010.

Where XXL's own reporting gets fuzzy is the date, and it is worth flagging rather than smoothing over. One XXL piece places the first use around 1999, in the video for "Blood Money Pt. 3," of all things while ordering from a Chinese restaurant. Another XXL piece says he coined it in the early 2000s and introduced it on *God's Favorite*, his June 25, 2002 album for Def Jam whose opening track is titled "Hit Me Slime."

Two articles, same publication, three-year gap. We are not going to pretend one of them is definitive. The attribution to N.O.R.E. is consistent across both. The debut is not.

The word then got a second New York push from Vado, the Harlem rapper who broke through after joining Cam'ron's group The U.N. in 2009 and used slime constantly as an ad-lib. XXL notes that Vado picked the word up secondhand rather than from N.O.R.E. directly, which is the ordinary way slang actually travels. Lil Wayne folded it into his vocabulary around 2011, which is roughly the moment a regional word becomes a national one.

## The Atlanta chapter: Young Thug and King Slime

Young Thug started recording in 2010 and founded Young Stoner Life Records in 2016 with 300 Entertainment. Everything about the label's branding ran through this one word.

The label's 2018 compilation *Slime Language* debuted at No. 8 on the Billboard 200 with 41,000 album-equivalent units. *Slime Language 2* went to No. 1 in April 2021. Wikipedia lists King Slime among his stage names, alongside Thugger, Spider and Sex. The snake became the visual shorthand, to the point that he announced *Slime Language* by mailing music publications live pet snakes with a booklet of the artists involved.

Lil Keed gave XXL the most quotable definition anyone has managed: slime is "something that's in you, not on you," and living up to it means you "have to be a leader." That is a long way from a synonym for friend. Somewhere between Queens and Atlanta the word picked up a value system.

The staying power is real. Future put a track called "Slimed In" on *We Don't Trust You* in 2024, six years after Young Thug used the same title on *Slime Language*. Slang usually has a two-year shelf life. This one is working on its third decade.

Stacking identity into a stage name is a rap tradition with a deep bench, and King Slime sits in the same lineage as the alter egos we catalog in our guides to [where rapper nicknames come from](/rapper-nicknames-explained) and [Drake's long list of aliases](/drake-nicknames).

## How slime escaped rap entirely

Ask a fifteen-year-old on a group chat what slime means and you will get a shrug and a correct usage. That is the endpoint of every successful slang term: total detachment from its source.

It made the jump for boring structural reasons. It is one syllable. It types fast. It has a ready-made emoji. And it works as a greeting, a label and a compliment without changing form, which means you can use it correctly without knowing anything about where it came from.

The same thing happened to the derived interjection, which is covered in full in our breakdown of [what "slatt" means](/what-does-slatt-mean). Wiktionary records slatt as an acronym of "slime love all the time" or "slime life all the time," making it a direct descendant. Most people now encounter the child before the parent.

## What slime does not mean

A few clarifications, because the search results for this word are unusually noisy.

It is not an acronym. Unlike slatt, slime is just the English noun, repurposed. Anyone selling you a backronym for it is making it up.

It is not interchangeable with slatt. Slime is a noun. Slatt is an interjection. You can have a slime. You say slatt.

And it is not a regional exclusive anymore. It started in New York, got its biggest amplifier in Atlanta, and now belongs to whoever says it. That path from one crew's inside word to a term with No. 1 albums attached is the same path most rap vocabulary takes, and it is why the words are worth tracking.
